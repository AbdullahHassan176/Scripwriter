### CLIP 4 · SECTION 5 — honest framing + three levers (one take, card on screen)

Mandela is not the villain in this story. He inherited a wrecked economy with investors at the door and corporate South Africa pushing austerity. Steadying the ship under that pressure was a real achievement.

But the bet was growth first, redistribution later — and thirty years on, you can see what it cost. The voters in those 1994 queues wanted jobs, land, and dignity in their own lifetime.

So what would it actually take to finish what Mandela promised?

First, land that moves for real — farms changing hands, counted and published every year, so that eleven percent figure cannot hide behind targets on paper.

Second, government tenders open before contracts are signed — so connected companies cannot loot the state before anyone else gets a bid in.

Third, state companies run by people who know the job — engineers and accountants at Eskom and Transnet, not party loyalists placed there because cadre deployment made loyalty the qualification.

That is the work postponed in 1996. South Africa is still waiting.
