**Description:**
```
On April 27th, 1994, twenty million South Africans voted for the first time.
The world watched and called it a miracle.

Most people say South Africa failed because of corruption. That's not the
answer. The real answer goes back to a deal made in 1994 — before the
looting, before the blackouts, before the food riots.

In 1994, the ANC had a plan — the RDP — to double the income of South
Africa's poorest 40 percent. Two years later, under pressure from the
country's fifty largest corporations, they scrapped it.

That choice — to change the government without changing who owns the
economy — is the story this video tells.

Thirty years later: the highest youth unemployment of any major economy
(61% for ages 15–24). Land reform at 11% of a 30% target. Black South
Africans owning 4% of private rural land. And in July 2021, the worst
violence since apartheid — where people looted food, not jewellery.

The South African Human Rights Commission — the government's own body —
found that the rioters' lives "resembled their colonial and apartheid
origins" as of 1981.

This is not a South African story. It is a story about what happens when
any democracy changes the politics but leaves the economics untouched.
```
