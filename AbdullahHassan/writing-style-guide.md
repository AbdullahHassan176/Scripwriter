## Three Registers (Know Which One You’re In)

| Register | When | Core move |
|----------|------|-----------|
| **Academic / actuarial** | Essays, research papers, assignments | Define terms → state research questions → literature → evidence (tables, metrics) → limitations → references |
| **Presentation / seminar** | Group seminars, oral exams | Framework first (e.g. control cycle) → teach background → dissect paper → critique methods → audience polls & humour |
| **Video / documentary** | YouTube flagship | Human cold open → structural thesis → timeline + data → plain-language layers → literary close |

Academic and video writing share the same **intellectual habits** (systems thinking, South Africa in global comparison, scepticism of single-cause explanations). They differ in **sentence rhythm and formality** — see each section below.

## Who I Am on Camera

- **Persona:** Regional expert in African and emerging-market economies — not a pundit, not a journalist doing hot takes.
- **Tone:** Authoritative but conversational. Measured energy that builds; flat and confident on thesis lines, not performative outrage.
- **Primary format:** Long-form documentary explainer (15–20 min), styled after **2 and 20** and **Qaanitah Hunter** — date/person cold open, structural “But…” flip, literary close with a historical quote. For Qaanitah-style drafts, see [`qaanitah-hunter-voice.md`](qaanitah-hunter-voice.md).
- **Audience default:** **International** — viewers may know nothing about the country. Explain every local term, acronym, and institution on first use.

## Core Beliefs (What Every Script Should Do)

1. **Break a lazy headline** — deliver on the title with a **specific human story**, not a lecture the audience already knows. Example: *Nelson Mandela's Biggest Regret* = Mandela's promise → his 1996 choice → what it cost — not “everyone knows the economic transition failed.”
2. **Use the full timeline** — bad outcomes that span 30 years are not explained by one president or one scandal.
3. **Separate layers** — bottom (structural deal), middle (institutions over decades), top (acute corruption/scandal). Never treat the top layer as the whole story.
4. **Earn specificity** — named people, dates, numbers, and sources. Vague claims sound like opinion; specifics sound like journalism.
5. **Deliver on the title in the first 60 seconds** — then escalate with data and narrative.

## Script Structure (Default Arc)

| Act | What happens | Reference |
|-----|----------------|-----------|
| **Cold open** | Hyper-specific **date + place + human moment** (queues, a person, a vote) | 2and20 Act 1 |
| **Contradiction flip** | “The world called it a miracle. Thirty years later…” / “They say it’s corruption. That’s not the answer.” | 2and20 Act 2 |
| **Title card** | *“I am Abdullah, a regional expert in African economies. And this is [Country]: [subtitle].”* | 2and20 Act 3 |
| **History / structure** | How the problem was **built** — policy forks (e.g. RDP → GEAR), ownership, inequality, institutions | Acts 4–5 |
| **Sponsor** | Thematically tied to the argument — not a random pivot | 2and20 Act 5 |
| **Mid sub-nudge** | Short, honest subscribe line when viewers are deep in the story | Optional |
| **Close** | Reframe + **historical quote** + hold on defining image (e.g. Sandton–Alexandra) | 2and20 Act 7 |

Sections should be labelled clearly: `[HOOK]`, `[SECTION 1 — …]`, etc., with **timeline cues** so editors and viewers know where they are (`[SHOW: timeline — 1996 highlighted]`).

**Format flexibility by topic type:**

The 2and20 defaults (date/place/person cold open · documentary subtitle title card · thematic sponsor) are optimised for geopolitical and historical topics where a named event or person anchors the story. For cultural analysis, psychology, or social trend pieces, these may be adapted:

- **Cold open:** May open on a statistic, a parallel trend, or a personal statement rather than a hard date/place/person — provided it sets a clear contradiction within the first 60 seconds. What must remain: a hook that creates an unanswered question the rest of the video resolves.
- **Title card:** May take a question format ("Is gaming making us more depressed?") rather than the documentary subtitle format when the thesis is genuinely unresolved at the point of the reveal. Choose whichever creates more tension at that beat.
- **Sponsor:** Required when monetising. When absent (early videos, non-monetised content), leave a `[SPONSOR PLACEHOLDER]` in the script at the 5–7 minute mark so the structural slot is maintained even if the read is not yet recorded.

What must always remain regardless of topic type: the **"But…" flip** · the **mid-subscribe nudge** before a key reveal · the **literary close** with a primary source quote held on screen.

## Voice & Sentence Rules

### Do

- Write **full sentences** for spoken delivery. Short sentences for punches; longer ones to carry complexity, then release into clarity.
- Write **for the ear** — read aloud mentally; if it doesn’t flow spoken, rewrite it.
- **Explain on first use:** apartheid, ANC, Eskom, rand, load shedding, cadre deployment, Gini, etc.
- Open sections with **where we are on the timeline** and what the viewer needs to know before the next beat.
- Use **concrete images** in narration that match what’s on screen: “Look at this chart…”, “Twenty million voted…”, “Food. Baby formula. Cooking oil.”
- Land thesis lines **once** — no hedging, no repetition of the same punchline.
- End sections with a **micro-hook** into the next (question, contrast, or “But underneath…”).
- **Show** why something is significant (chart, number, image) — don’t **tell** the audience it “matters” in a throwaway line.
- **Sound spoken on camera** — oral pivots where needed: *right?*, *If only it was that simple*, *That being said*, *Yeah, those…* (see [`edit-patterns-from-sa-script.md`](edit-patterns-from-sa-script.md)).
- **Use rhetorical question ladders for social silence** — when explaining why a stigma, behaviour pattern, or public silence persists, build a short cascade of genuine questions ("Is it because…?", "And if that cost is real, why…?", "Do men actually need permission…?") rather than stating the cause declaratively. Close the ladder with one plain declarative that lands the weight. See `edit-patterns-from-sa-script.md` §13.
- **Teach big policy forks out loud** — frame the setup (e.g. power transfer between parties), then **Option A / Option B**, then which was chosen — don’t jump to *“They chose to keep the structure”* in one sentence.
- **TH sign-off includes the show title** — *“I am Abdullah… This is [Title].”* (2and20-style).

### Don’t

- **No essay bridges before data** — cut lines like *“the numbers tell a different story”*; go from *“If only it was that simple”* (or similar) straight into the chart.
- **No essay validation lines** — cut narrator lines that **grade** a quote or moment instead of continuing the story: *“That was not a slogan for the campaign trail”*, *“the liberation movement had carried for decades”*, *“at the centre of his legacy”*, *“would define everything that came after”*. State the fact in plain speech: *“The ANC had been saying that for forty years”*, *“Those people were voting for jobs and land”*. See [`avoid-ai-sounding-writing.md`](avoid-ai-sounding-writing.md) §4.5b.
- **No faux-contrast emphasis** — banned in three forms: (1) em-dash flip: *“That is not destiny — it is unfinished business”*; (2) explicit contrast: *“X is not Y, it is Z”*; (3) split-sentence contrast: two short punchy sentences where the second negates or qualifies the first (*“COVID did not create that number. It just made it impossible to deny.”*). All three are the same AI rhythm. If sentence B qualifies sentence A, merge them into one. Say it plainly: *“After thirty years, a gap like that is still a policy choice.”* See void-ai-sounding-writing.md §4.1–4.1c.
- **No “Treat X as Y, not Z” / listicle commands** — avoid *“Treat land reform as delivery, not slogans”*, *“Hold the coalition to…”*, *“Use the institutions…”*. Say what people can do in normal speech: *“One place to start is…”*, *“On land, the gap was never only about…”*.
- **No “that matters” / “this matters” as narrator filler** — you do not need to keep telling people something matters. If it matters, **prove it** in the next sentence (data, contrast, consequence). Cut standalone lines like *“That matters. But the structural problems…”* → go straight to *“But the structural problems…”* or fold the stakes into the line (*“Rolling blackouts largely stopped — but who owns the land still hasn’t shifted.”*). Same ban on *“And that matters.”*, *“Which is why this matters.”*, *“Here’s why that matters.”* stacked across a script. **Exception:** rare, specific clause where “matters” names a **trade-off** (*“cleaner government matters — but it is not the whole fix”*) — once per piece at most, not as a tic.
- **No emphatic `did` + verb** when simple past is enough — write *“The economy grew”*, not *“The economy did grow”*. Same for *failed*, *changed*, *improve*, etc. Reserve *did* for **contrast or negation** (*“GDP did not grow”*, *“growth did happen, but only in headline terms”*) — not as a default way to state a past fact.
- **No fragment chains** for rhetorical effect — avoid staccato lists like “Corruption. Zuma. Done.” or inspirational trailer cuts like “Five hundred metres. Two worlds. One city.” Use connected prose even in the climax.
- No generic openers: “In today’s video…”, “Welcome back…”, “Let’s dive in…”
- No filler closers: “In conclusion,” “To summarise,” “As you can see”
- No padding — every sentence must earn its place.
- Don’t blame a single villain when the data spans multiple presidents or decades.
- Don’t assume the audience knows South African (or local) politics, geography, or party names without a one-line context.
- Don’t write **more formal** than how I speak on camera — not academic paper voice unless quoting research.

## Visual & Production Conventions

When visuals matter (default for flagship scripts), use:

```
[SHOW: description]  = on screen now; narration speaks to what viewers see
[SHOW: timeline — years highlighted]  = orientation on a timeline bar
```

**Delivery tags** (for filming / Word sync):

| Tag | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| `[VO]` | Voiceover — not on camera; B-roll or data card |
| `[TH]` | Talking head — on camera, direct to lens |
| `[TH→]` | Talking head that cuts to B-roll mid-sentence |
| `[SP]` | Sponsor read |
| `[SUB]` | Subscribe bump |
| `[PAUSE]` | Hold / beat (especially before or after a thesis line) |

**Talking head discipline:** Film all `[TH]` in one session. Use TH for **thesis declarations**, naming the deal, and key reframes — not for every paragraph. Target ~5–6 min TH in a 15–20 min video; rest is VO + visuals.

**Data & graphics:** Prefer **data cards**, timelines, and comparison charts over talking-head exposition for numbers. Hold stark stats on screen (e.g. Gini 0.63 for several seconds).

## Content & Research Standards

- **Central narrative** must be stated in the production package: one sentence thesis + what assumption we break.
- Cite or list **key data sources** at the end of the script (IEC, Stats SA, World Bank, commissions, etc.).
- Match claims to **production package shot list** where possible — script lines should map to visuals.
- Sponsor segments must **connect to the video’s argument** (privacy/state power, business chaos vs dysfunctional government, etc.).

## Pacing & Length

| Target | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| **Runtime** | 15–20 min flagship; state estimated runtime and word count in header |
| **Words** | ~150–160 WPM spoken → ~2,400 words ≈ 15–16 min |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Every ~90–120 seconds: new chart, TH cut, contrast, or timeline jump |
| **Midpoint insight** | The most shareable/clippable line should land around the middle |
| **If cutting** | Trim montage lists and repeated recap layers before cutting cold open or thesis |

## Hook Patterns That Work for Me

1. **Date + place + human detail** — “April 27th, 1994. South Africa. The queues began before sunrise.”
2. **Miracle → decay** — honour what was achieved, then ask what happened since.
3. **Common explanation → wrong** — “If corruption is the whole explanation, the fix sounds simple…”
4. **Chart that spans the whole era** — inequality flat across Zuma and non-Zuma years; youth unemployment rising across presidents.

## Difficulty + Tangible Levers (How to Close a Structural Story)

When the thesis is “the fix is not simple,” do **both** in one section — never stop at difficulty, never jump to vague hope.

### 1. Name why change is hard **using causes already in the video**

Tie to your layers: 1996 bargain, capital flight, patronage state, coalition politics, unemployment pressure. One **honest TH beat**: *“none of this is a switch a president flips in one term.”*

### 2. Give **concrete** things South Africans can push toward

Say it like advice to a smart friend, not a NGO briefing deck. No *“Treat X as Y, not Z.”*

Not “hold leaders accountable” — specify:

| Lever | Tangible example |
|-------|------------------|
| **Electoral / coalition pressure** | Public scorecard on land %, youth unemployment, published contracts |
| **Institutions** | Courts, press, Public Protector — link scandals to procurement rules, cadre deployment, SOE appointments |
| **Procurement** | Open bidding and audit on state spend |
| **Land** | Lawful transfers + post-transfer support; measure hectares and output |
| **Private sector** | Real ownership and hiring, not tick-box BEE reports |

### 3. Voice rules for this block

- Full sentences; no *“not destiny — it is…”* emphasis tricks  
- No empty *“that matters”*  
- End the block with **organising logic** (*contracts, tenders, hectares*) — then inspirational close (queues, Mandela, Sandton)

See `generatedScripts/SA Captured Rainbow/dark_side_of_south_africa_script.md` §5 for the reference implementation.

## Close Patterns That Work for Me

- **Three-layer recap** in plain language (structural → institutional → scandal).
- **Reframe line:** e.g. “Political freedom ≠ economic freedom.”
- **Return to opening image** (1994 queues) — what they voted for vs what they got.
- **Historical quote** (Mandela, etc.) — short, white on black, `[PAUSE]` before or after.
- **Soft CTA:** “Thank you for watching. If this helped you see the story differently, subscribe…”

- ❌ Imitate ultra-short creators (h1t1, DougSharpe) for my flagship Africa/economics pieces
- ❌ Strip `[SHOW:]` / production markers when I’m in visual-script workflow
- ❌ Add B-roll or stage directions **only** when I’m doing a **narration-only** draft (see `ai.md`: default is no visuals unless asked — **my** Abdullah scripts are the exception when I request full production format)

- ❌ Clickbait thesis that the script doesn’t prove in the first third
- ❌ Invent statistics — flag gaps and ask for sources

## Quick Checklist Before Calling a Draft Done

- [ ] International audience: every local term explained once
- [ ] Full sentences; no fragment chains; simple past (*grew*) not filler *did grow*
- [ ] No empty *that matters* / *this matters* — stakes shown, not announced
- [ ] Spoken pivots where needed; Section 1–level ideas use Option A/B or questions, not one compressed paragraph
- [ ] TH line ends with *This is [Title]* — or, for social/cultural topics, a question format that opens the thesis rather than stating it
- [ ] Thesis clear by end of hook; title card spoken
- [ ] Timeline spans full period, not one administration
- [ ] Three layers (structure / institutions / scandal) if applicable
- [ ] TH only on thesis beats; VO carries story and data
- [ ] `[SHOW:]` aligned with production package where it exists
- [ ] Sources listed; runtime and word count in header
- [ ] Close: reframe + quote + return image

## Academic & Professional Writing (Summary)

> Full detail: [`academic-writing-profile.md`](academic-writing-profile.md) — structure, sentence stats, openers you hate, seminar voice.

### Structural fingerprints

- **Numbered hierarchy:** `1.` → `1.1` → `1.1.1` (and `¶3.5`-style cross-references in long papers). Sections are explicit: INTRODUCTION, AIM, METHODOLOGY, DISCUSSION, REFERENCES.
- **Front-loaded rigour:** Abstract, keywords, contact block; **research question in quotes** in shorter pieces; **numbered research questions** as bullet lists (`−`) in full papers.
- **Plan of development:** Tell the reader the roadmap before the body (what comes in §4, §5, what metrics will be used, that limitations will be addressed).
- **Comparative design:** South Africa vs a **diverse basket of countries** (LMIC + HIC), “allowing for notable exceptions” — not cherry-picked winners.
- **Evidence via tables:** Country metrics, indicator definitions (e.g. UHC Service Coverage Index, OOPS, CHE per capita), then interpretation in prose — don’t bury numbers in paragraphs alone.
- **Policy anatomy:** Financing → pooling → purchasing → benefit structures; reforms named precisely (NHI Bill, PHC re-engineering, RDP/GEAR parallels in video work).

### Argumentation habits

- **Define before using:** UHC, NHI, medical schemes, fragmented risk pools, two-tier system — same instinct as “explain every term” on YouTube.
- **Literature as dialogue:** “The literature identifies…”, “The approaches taken are appropriate **yet**…”, “agrees upon the points suggested **and suggests**…” — agree, then extend.
- **Layered causation:** Inefficiencies at pooling, allocation, governance — not one villain (mirrors video’s structural / institutional / scandal layers).
- **Honest scope:** Flag indicator limitations, need for further interviews/investigation, “preliminary answer” when evidence is incomplete.
- **Actuarial framing:** Risk pools, catastrophic expenditure, control cycle, endogenous vs exogenous factors (seminar: Warren Buffett / operational vs external risk analogy).
- **South African anchor, global lens:** Post-apartheid health transformation, NHI, Stats SA / WHO / Lancet citations — international comparison is **for policy insight**, not tourism.

### Sentence-level (academic)

**Do**

- Use **formal but clear** prose: subordinate clauses are fine; precision over punchiness.
- Prefer **“Furthermore,” “However,” “Thus,” “It is proposed that”** where appropriate — not forbidden in academic register (unlike video).
- Use **em-dash bullet lists** for challenges, reforms, or multi-part plans.
- Cite **in-text (Author, year)** consistently; long reference lists are normal.
- Sign short pieces **“By A Hassan”**; full name on formal contact blocks.

**Don’t (academic)**

- Don’t state conclusions without tying back to the **stated research question**.
- Don’t use seminar humour, polls, or pop-culture asides (Infinity Gauntlet, flat-earth jokes) — save for presentation register.
- Don’t treat correlation as proof; flag **data gathering / indicator construction** limits when relevant.
- Don’t omit **limitations and future work** — you routinely include them (practice essay §3.2, research draft abstract, seminar critique block).

### Presentation / seminar voice (`Script 2.0.docx`)

Distinct from essays — still **structured and analytical**, but **conversational and performative**:

- Open with **agenda + learning outcomes** (“At the end of this seminar, we want you to be able to answer…”).
- Use a **familiar framework** as spine (Actuarial Control Cycle: problem → past solutions → results → monitor limitations).
- **Handoffs** between speakers (“Abdullah will now…”, “Back to you, Abdullah”).
- **Rhetorical questions** and **polls**; light humour and self-awareness (camera off, “Steve, coffee break”).
- **Analogies** to make abstract models stick (Kelvin quote, Hamurabi → performance measurement, actuarial risk vs climate exogeneity).
- **Hyper-critical close:** sample size, thematic analysis bias, “amorphous” paper structure — then balanced takeaways.

When drafting **video** narration, borrow seminar strengths (clear roadmap, explicit questions, analogies) — not the jokes or meta-commentary unless asked.

### Topics you return to (academic → video pipeline)

- Universal Health Coverage, NHI, two-tier / fragmented systems, inequality of spend (public vs private).
- Institutional design: who pools, who allocates, who governs.
- COVID-19 as **stress test** of system design (not just a health story).
- Corruption as **accelerant**, not root cause (COVID funds abuse in research draft; Zuma layer in video scripts).

## How University Style Maps to YouTube Scripts

| Academic habit | Video expression |
|----------------|------------------|
| Numbered research questions | “So in this video I am going to answer…” / section promises |
| Country comparison tables | `[SHOW: data card]` + timeline |
| “Literature agrees… yet…” | “They say it’s corruption. That’s not the answer.” |
| Plan of development | Hook roadmap + `[SHOW: timeline]` |
| Limitations paragraph | Three-layer recap + honest “still waiting for the rest” |
| Define UHC / NHI / schemes | International audience — explain every term |
| Formal “holistic perspective” | Plain language: “walk you through the thirty years” |

**Keep video different:** Shorter sentences on camera, no `1.1.1` numbering in narration, no parenthetical citation chains — sources go in footer `KEY DATA SOURCES`, not mid-sentence `(Coovadia et al., 2009)`.
