# Avoiding AI-Sounding Writing — Abdullah Hassan

> What to cut, rewrite, or never generate — so drafts sound like **you**, not default ChatGPT.  
> Built from: your [`writing-style-guide.md`](writing-style-guide.md) + [`academic-writing-profile.md`](academic-writing-profile.md), plus common reader/editor tells (r/WritingWithAI community threads on overused AI phrasing, em dashes, and formatting; James Presbitero’s “7 signs” framework on emotionally flat, pattern-heavy prose).

**Important:** Many “AI tells” are also used by humans. Em dashes, “holistic”, or “However” appear in **your** honours papers legitimately. This doc is about **density, defaults, and combinations** — not banning a word once.

---

## 1. Why This Doc Exists

- Readers, editors, and detectors flag **lazy, unedited AI** — flat tone, same phrases everywhere, fake depth.
- You’ve worried you might “write like AI” (e.g. overusing *game changer*). Your **pre-2021 university work** does *not* read like slop: numbered structure, citations, qualified claims, no *delve* spam.
- **Risk today:** AI drafts + light touch-up = generic voice. Fix is **edit toward your profile**, not avoid every word on a meme list.

**Rule:** If a phrase could appear in any essay about any country on any topic, delete it.

---

## 2. The “Same Guy Wrote Every Paragraph” Problem

AI default voice is:

- Technically correct, emotionally flat  
- Same rhythm every paragraph (medium sentence → transition → medium sentence)  
- Same transitions (*Furthermore*, *Moreover*, *In conclusion*)  
- No stakes, no specific human cost, no awkward honesty  

**Your antidote (always steer edits here):**

| AI default | Your voice |
|------------|------------|
| Vague importance | Date + place + named person |
| “The situation is complex” | Three layers: structure → institutions → scandal |
| Hype adjectives | Stats, sources, timeline |
| “Let’s dive in” | Cold open or *“The purpose of this paper is to answer…”* |
| Symmetrical “not X, but Y” every paragraph | *appropriate **yet***, *agrees upon… and suggests* (sparingly) |
| Poetic filler | Food on the floor, queues, Sandton–Alexandra — concrete |

## 3. Word & Phrase Blacklist (High Confidence AI Tells)

Cut or rewrite on sight unless you **meant** it in dialogue or a direct quote.

### Tier A — scream “ChatGPT” in 2024–2026 output

| Word / phrase | Why it flags | Your replacement habit |
|---------------|--------------|------------------------|
| **delve** (into) | Over-trained meme word | *examine*, *anatomise*, *walk through*, *look at* |
| **game changer** | Reddit/YouTube callout; hype | Name the **mechanism** that changed (1996 GEAR fork, etc.) |
| **landscape** (figurative) | “The economic landscape…” | *structure*, *who owned what*, *system* |
| **tapestry** | Empty texture | Delete |
| **testament to** | Fake profundity | *shows*, *is evidence of* + source |
| **underscores / highlights** (as verb) | AI summary voice | *shows*, *the data on this chart…* |
| **pivotal / crucial / vital** (stacked) | Padding | Cut or replace with number |
| **foster / leverage / harness** | Consultant-speak | *build*, *use*, *shift* |
| **realm** | Fantasy filler | *area*, *sector*, or be specific |
| **multifaceted** | Hides lack of facets | List the 2–3 facets |
| **in today’s [fast-paced/digital] world** | Throat-clearing | Start with your hook |
| **it’s important to note that** | AI hedge | Just state the note |
| **in conclusion** (mid-essay) | You already hate this | Section heading or pivot sentence |
| **as an AI** / **I hope this helps** | Obvious | Never |
| **navigate** (challenges) | Coaching cliché | *face*, *deal with*, or name policy |
| **robust** (unless statistical) | Buzzword | *durable*, *well-governed*, or cut |
| **seamless** | Marketing | Cut |
| **cutting-edge** | Marketing | Cut |

### Tier B — often AI (use rarely; you use some formally)

| Word / phrase | Note for you |
|---------------|--------------|
| **palpable** | Reddit flag; rare in your PDFs — avoid in scripts |
| **enigmatic / pragmatic** | Instant red flag in fiction; odd in your essays |
| **hitch** (and hitching) | Reported ChatGPT tic — you don’t use it |
| **we are in this together** | Motivational AI |
| **physical** + abstract noun (*physical blow, physical weight*) | Claude pattern — use only for literal physics |
| **long shadows** (repeated) | Literary AI tic — once per piece max |
| **the silence was satisfied** / objects “remembering” | Personification spam — not your register |
| **whispered / echoed / danced** (verbs on ideas) | Creative AI — not documentary voice |

### Tier C — yours is OK; AI overuses them

| Word / phrase | Your usage | AI abuse pattern |
|---------------|------------|------------------|
| **holistic** | Honours papers — legitimate | Every paragraph |
| **However / Furthermore / Additionally** | Academic PDFs — measured | Every sentence start |
| **Thus / Therefore** | Seminar + papers | Chain of three+ in a row |
| **comprehensive** | Your formal register | Hollow “comprehensive guide” titles |
| **Furthermore** | Low count in your work | Stacked with Moreover, Additionally |

## 4. Structural Patterns That Sound Like AI

### 4.1 “Not X, but Y” / “X is not Y — it is Z” (Abdullah hates this)

**Banned emphasis pattern:**

```text
That distance is not destiny — it is unfinished business.
```

Sounds like ChatGPT trying to sound profound. Prefer **plain spoken cause**:

```text
After thirty years, a gap like that is still a policy choice.
```

| AI contrast | More natural |
|-------------|----------------|
| *is not destiny — it is unfinished business* | *is still a policy choice* / *was never finished* |
| *is not just X — it is Y* | *X helps, but the real work is Y* |
| *is not a nation out of fight* | *have not run out of fight yet* |
| *did not die — it lives on* | *is still alive in* |
| *does not need X — it needs Y* | *has had enough X. The country still needs Y* |
| *Treat land reform as delivery, not slogans* | *On land, progress shows up when transfers speed up…* |
| *Hold the coalition to… / Use the institutions…* | *One place to start is… / Courts and journalists still…* |

Use **one** clean contrast per section if needed (*“They say corruption. That’s not the answer.”*). Do not stack the em-dash formula.

### 4.1b “Not X, but Y” / contrast stacking (general)

Community + Presbitero-adjacent pattern: AI loves symmetrical reframes.

```text
BAD (AI rhythm):
South Africa is not a failure of democracy, but a failure of economics.
It is not about corruption alone, but about structure.
It is not a crisis of intent, but of design.
```

```text
BETTER (your rhythm — one turn, then proof):
They say it's corruption. That's not the answer.
If corruption is the whole explanation, the fix sounds simple…
[SHOW: timeline — inequality flat across Zuma and non-Zuma years]
```

**Rule:** One strong flip per section. Proof immediately after.

### 4.2 Staccato “literary” fragments

AI (and bad editors) produce:

```text
Corruption. Zuma. State capture. Done.
```

You explicitly **banned** this for video; academic you use **full sentences** and `1)` `2)` lists — not fake poetry.

### 4.3 Short sentence chains + abstract nouns

Reddit pattern: *“The grassfield is not a place, it's an ideal. Distances shorten. Colours become vivid.”*

- Sounds profound, says nothing  
- You need **verifiable claims** (Gini 0.63, 61% youth unemployment, 11% land reform)

### 4.4 Redundant phrasing in one sentence

> “delve” + **double-stating the same idea** in one line.

```text
BAD: It is important to note that NHI fundamentally represents a crucial pivotal shift in the healthcare landscape.
GOOD: The NHI Bill proposes a single purchaser for all registered citizens (NDoH, 2019).
```

### 4.5 Generic section openers

| AI opener | Your opener |
|-----------|-------------|
| In today’s video we will explore… | April 27th, 1994. South Africa. |
| Let’s dive into the fascinating world of… | To understand 1994, you need to know what came before. |
| This article will discuss… | The purpose of this paper is to answer the following: "…" |
| Without further ado… | (delete) |

### 4.6 Over-metaphoring

ChatGPT **loves** metaphors and giving objects feelings. Your seminar uses **one** controlled joke metaphor (infinity gauntlet for four BSC quadrants) — not every paragraph.

**Documentary:** Metaphor from **real images** (queues, food looting, aerial Sandton–Alexandra) — not “the economy whispered.”

### 4.7 Irrelevant sensory detail

AI pads with meal descriptions, weather, “enigmatic smiles” when the story is policy. If the visual doesn’t support the thesis, cut it.

### 4.8 Inconsistent facts (AI hallucination tell)

Same week: sister is 23, then 19, then 30; school vs home — readers assume AI or no editor. **You** anchor dates, presidents, percentages — keep that discipline when using AI for research drafts.

## 5. Formatting & Copy-Paste Tells

### 5.1 Em dashes (—)

- **Reddit/Medium:** ChatGPT overuses em dashes vs hyphen `-`.  
- **You:** Use them in seminar; academic PDFs less. **Problem is density** — three em dashes per paragraph.

**Edit rule:**

- Max **1–2 em dashes per 500 words** in final prose unless quoting.  
- Prefer: comma, full stop, or parentheses.  
- Windows: em dash is Alt+0151 — don’t let paste replace your normal punctuation habit.

### 5.2 Markdown bleed from chat UI

Pasting from ChatGPT/Claude often brings:

- `**bold**` or `###` headers in a Word doc  
- Bullet `•` mixed with `-`  
- Trailing spaces, odd line breaks  
- `[link](url)` syntax  
- Horizontal rules `---`

**Fix:** Paste into Notepad or “Paste without formatting” → then into Word/script.md. Your `build_visual_script.py` pipeline expects **clean markdown**, not chat styling.

### 5.3 “Hidden code” in text?

- **Word .docx** has XML you don’t see — normal, not proof of AI.  
- **Chat exports** don’t embed executable code in plain text; risk is **metadata** in PDF generators, not moral “AI fingerprints” in `.txt`.  
- **Real issue:** Amazon/platforms use **detectors + style**, not hidden Unicode in 99% of cases.  
- **Unicode gotchas to strip:** zero-width spaces, NBSP, smart quotes if your toolchain breaks. Search `\u200b`, replace fancy quotes if needed.

### 5.4 Perfect typography

AI output often has:

- Curly quotes everywhere  
- Uniform em dashes  
- No typos ever  

**Your authentic academic PDFs** have occasional spacing artifacts (`h ave`, `all ocation`) — human. **Don’t** mistake “too polished” as professional; mistake **too generic** as AI.

## 6. The Seven Sign Categories (Presbitero-style — mapped to you)

Use as an editing pass after any AI-assisted draft.

| # | Sign | What it looks like | Your fix |
|---|------|-------------------|----------|
| 1 | **Emotionally flat** | Correct but no stakes | Add human cost (queues, food, unemployment dial) |
| 2 | **Generic importance** | “Crucial moment in history” | Date + name + number |
| 3 | **Predictable structure** | Intro → 3 bullets → conclusion | Your act arc: hook → flip → title card → sections → quote close |
| 4 | **AI vocabulary cluster** | delve + landscape + robust in one page | Tier A blacklist scan |
| 5 | **Contrast spam** | Not X but Y × 5 | One flip + evidence |
| 6 | **Fake literary** | Shadows, stones remembering rain | Concrete B-roll / data cards |
| 7 | **No accountable claims** | No sources, round vibes | IEC, Stats SA, WHO — footer + in-text |

## 7. What You Already Do Right (Don’t Panic)

From your **pre-AI** corpus — these are **anti-AI signals**:

- Quoted research questions; numbered `1.1.1` sections  
- `(Author, year)` citations — not “studies show”  
- **Preliminary answer** / **limitations** / **future work**  
- *appropriate yet*, *agrees upon* — not *game changer*  
- Country tables with defined indicators  
- COVID section ties corruption to **exacerbation**, not lone cause  
- Seminar: real methodology critique (sample size, saturation, thematic bias)  
- Video: ban on fragment chains; international glossaries  

If an editor flags you, point to **specificity and structure** — not vocabulary paranoia alone.

## 9. Editing Workflow (AI Draft → Sounds Like Abdullah)

1. **Generate** with [`writing-style-guide.md`](writing-style-guide.md) + [`academic-writing-profile.md`](academic-writing-profile.md) in context — not vanilla prompts.  
2. **Paste plain** — strip markdown/chat formatting.  
3. **Blacklist pass** — Tier A words → delete or replace (search: `delve`, `landscape`, `testament`, `underscore`, `game changer`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `navigate`, `robust`, `seamless`).  
4. **Em dash pass** — replace 70%+ with commas or full stops.  
5. **Contrast pass** — keep best **one** “not X / real answer is Y” per section.  
6. **Specificity pass** — every abstract noun gets a number, date, or `[SHOW:]`.  
7. **Read aloud** — stumble = rewrite (your ear test from video guide).  
8. **Register check** — academic vs seminar vs script: openers match [`academic-writing-profile.md`](academic-writing-profile.md) §5–6.  
9. **Source pass** — claims without citations → footnote or cut.

## 10. Prompts for AI Assistants (Repo Use)

When generating for Abdullah, **never**:

- Stack Tier A blacklist words  
- Open with “In today’s…” / “Let’s dive…”  
- Close with “In conclusion” + summary of what you just said  
- Produce fragment-chain rhetoric  
- Add poetic personification of objects  
- Use “not X, but Y” more than once per 300 words  
- Emit `[SHOW:]`-irrelevant purple prose  
- Invent statistics (flag `[SOURCE NEEDED]` instead)

**Always:**

- Prefer your hooks (date, queue, person)  
- Three-layer logic where applicable  
- Full sentences in narration  
- Name institutions once, plainly  
- List sources in `KEY DATA SOURCES` or REFERENCES

## 11. Pre-Publish Checklist

**Vocabulary**

- [ ] Zero Tier A blacklist hits (or one intentional quote)  
- [ ] No *game changer*, *delve*, *landscape* (figurative), *testament to*  
- [ ] *palpable / enigmatic / pragmatic* absent unless quoting  

**Structure**

- [ ] No >1 symmetrical “not X, but Y” per section  
- [ ] No fragment-chain drama  
- [ ] No generic intro/outro  
- [ ] Metaphors ≤ 1 per major section (video); minimal in academic  

**Format**

- [ ] Pasted without chat markdown artifacts  
- [ ] Em dashes ≤ 2 per 500 words (adjust for register)  
- [ ] Facts dated and consistent (names, ages, places)  

**Voice**

- [ ] Sounds like **you** per register (academic / seminar / video)  
- [ ] At least one **undeniable specific** per 200 words (number, law, date, name)  
- [ ] Limitations or honest scope where claims are broad
